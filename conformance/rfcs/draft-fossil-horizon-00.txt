        The Fossil Distributed Version Control System: Horizons
                        draft-fossil-horizon-00

Abstract

   This document specifies horizons for repositories following the
   Fossil artifact model: a mechanism by which a clone deliberately
   holds the history of a repository only from a declared check-in
   forward.  It defines two cuts of increasing depth.  The content
   horizon holds every structural artifact of every age and elides only
   the file-content blobs that no check-in at or above the horizon
   references; the derivation of repository state is unchanged and every
   held artifact verifies by its own name.  The structural horizon
   additionally omits the check-in manifests below the horizon and
   stands a single origin-minted control artifact, the seed, in their
   place, so that the existing tag-propagation derivation yields correct
   branch and tag state for the history that is held.  Both cuts keep
   every artifact's real name, rewrite nothing, and heal toward a dense
   clone through ordinary synchronization.  The horizon is specified as
   a temporal member of the cone defined by the sparse-cone document, so
   that path sparseness and history sparseness compose.

1.  Introduction

1.1.  Two Cuts, Deliberately Separated

   A participant that works at the tip of a long history wants to pay
   for the tip, not for the history.  Two different savings are
   available and they have different prerequisites.

   The first is content.  File-content blobs are the bulk of a source
   repository's bytes, and a blob referenced only by check-ins older
   than the participant's interest will never be read.  Eliding those
   blobs saves most of the transfer and most of the disk while leaving
   every structural artifact in place, so every derived view (timeline,
   leaves, branch state, merge ancestry) is computed exactly as for a
   dense clone.  This is the content horizon of Section 4.  It requires
   nothing of the serving peer beyond what [FossilSparseCone] already
   requires, and it is correct against a peer that does not understand
   it.

   The second is structure.  Check-in manifests for all of history are
   proportional to the length of the history, and for a sufficiently
   long one they are themselves worth omitting.  Omitting them removes
   the input from which branch and tag state is derived, so something
   must stand in for the absent ancestry.  This is the structural
   horizon of Section 5.  It requires a cooperating origin, and it is
   specified so that what stands in for the ancestry is an ordinary
   control artifact whose effect the existing derivation already knows
   how to apply.

   This document keeps the two separate.  An implementation MAY provide
   the content horizon alone.  The structural horizon presupposes the
   content horizon.

1.2.  Real Names, Always

   A manifest's name is the hash of its bytes, and its P cards are among
   those bytes.  Rewriting a manifest to drop the parents it names would
   rename it and, transitively, every descendant.  This document never
   does that.  Every artifact a horizon clone holds is the artifact the
   dense repository holds, under the name the dense repository knows it
   by.  A check-in committed from a horizon clone is the check-in that
   lands at the origin, under one name, with no translation.  The
   ancestry a horizon clone lacks is represented exactly as
   [FossilRepoState] already represents content a store knows by name
   and does not hold: as phantoms.

1.3.  The Graph, Not the Log

   The horizon is a check-in C and a rule for which check-ins are held
   relative to C in the check-in graph.  It is not a byte offset into a
   store's log, not a count of check-ins, and not a timestamp.  Log
   order and timestamp order both differ from ancestry in any repository
   with concurrent branches, and a cut defined on either would hold a
   check-in while lacking a parent that is newer in the log or later in
   time.  Every rule in this document is stated over P cards.

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",

   capitals, as shown here.

2.  Terminology

   Horizon:  A check-in C, named by its artifact hash, together with a
      held-set rule (Section 3.1).  A horizon is local policy of one
      clone; it is not an artifact and does not synchronize.

   Held set (K):  The set of check-ins a horizon clone holds manifests
      and content for, computed from C by the held-set rule over the
      check-in graph.

   Above, below:  A check-in is above the horizon if it is in K and
      below the horizon otherwise.  An artifact is above the horizon if
      some check-in in K references it and below otherwise.



Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                 [Page 4]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   Content horizon:  A clone that holds every structural artifact of
      every age and elides the file-content blobs that lie below the
      horizon (Section 4).

   Structural horizon:  A clone that additionally omits the check-in
      manifests below the horizon and holds a seed in their place
      (Section 5).

   Seed:  A control artifact minted by the origin that records, on C,
      the propagating and non-propagating tag state in force at C in the
      dense repository (Section 5.2).

   Boundary hash:  The name of a check-in below the horizon that is a
      parent of a check-in in K.  Boundary hashes are the P-card
      references that cross the horizon.

   Deepen:  Move C to an ancestor, pull the manifests and content that
      the new K adds, and obtain a new seed when one is in use
      (Section 5.8).

   Origin:  The serving peer that understands this document's pragmas.
      A peer that does not is horizon-ignorant.

   Structural artifact:  As in [FossilSparseCone]: every artifact that
      is not a file-content blob -- check-in manifests, sub-manifest
      nodes, control artifacts, wiki, ticket, event, forum, attachment
      and cluster artifacts.

3.  The Horizon Specification

3.1.  The Horizon Reference and the Held Set

   A horizon names one check-in C by its full artifact hash.  The held
   set K is computed from C by one of two rules, which the horizon also
   names:

   descendants:  K is C and every check-in reachable from C by following
      P cards backward, that is, every descendant of C.

   leaves:  K is the descendants rule's set, plus, for every open leaf L
      in the repository, every check-in on a path from L back to the
      nearest common ancestor of L and C, inclusive of that ancestor.

   The descendants rule is the default.  Under the leaves rule, any two
   check-ins that are both open leaves, or both descendants of C, have
   their merge base in K, so a merge between live branches resolves
   without crossing the horizon (Section 5.5); its cost is every
   manifest from each open leaf back to its fork from C's ancestry,



Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                 [Page 5]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   which on a repository that keeps old branches open is several times
   the tip tree's content (Section 3.4).  Under the descendants rule a
   branch forked below C is held from its fork forward only if the fork
   is C itself, and a merge whose base is below C deepens first.  A
   client that expects to merge across live branches offline SHOULD
   choose the leaves rule knowingly.

   K is a function of C, the rule, and the repository's check-in graph.
   It changes when new check-ins arrive, and a horizon clone recomputes
   it from its held manifests; an origin recomputes it from the dense
   graph.  No party stores K.

3.2.  Location and Composition

   A horizon is store-adjacent local state, stored and classified
   exactly as the store cone of [FossilSparseCone] Section 3.1: it is
   not an artifact, it does not synchronize, and it affects no
   artifact's bytes or name.  Loss of the horizon reclassifies every
   artifact it elided as a transit phantom, and the store heals toward
   dense through ordinary synchronization; [FossilSparseCone]
   Section 5.2's rule that policy loss costs bandwidth and never data
   applies unchanged.

   A horizon is a member of the cone.  A store MAY hold a horizon, a
   path cone, both, or neither.  When both are held, the set of blobs
   kept is the intersection of the two keep sets: a blob is held if it
   is above the horizon AND named by an in-cone F card.  The structural
   set is governed by the horizon alone; path cones never elide
   structure.

3.3.  Canonical Serialization

   A horizon serializes into the canonical cone form of
   [FossilSparseCone] Section 3 as one member distinguished from path
   members by kind, carrying the hash of C and the rule name.  The
   serialization MUST be canonical so that the cone's digest remains a
   valid memoization key for the origin's per-request classification.  A
   cone with a horizon member MUST be marked with a version distinct
   from a cone without one, so that an implementation predating this
   document refuses rather than misreads it.

3.4.  Choosing C

   This section is informative.  The natural C is the tip of the branch
   the clone will work on, under the descendants rule: K is then that
   tip and whatever lands after it, and the clone holds one tree's
   content plus the manifests of its own future.  Measured over several
   long-lived repositories, that is a few percent of the dense store.



Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                 [Page 6]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   The leaves rule makes the merge-base guarantee hold for live work
   regardless of how C is chosen, and it is the right choice for a clone
   that will merge across branches offline.  Its cost is proportional to
   how far back open leaves fork: a repository with a handful of open
   release branches forked years ago holds every manifest on each of
   them, and on a flat-manifest repository those manifests can outweigh
   the tip tree's blobs several times over.  A good C under the leaves
   rule is the nearest common ancestor of every open leaf, which an
   origin can compute from its indexes and offer to a cloning client;
   this places the horizon as late as possible while keeping every live
   branch's whole divergence in K.  An origin that offers a default C
   SHOULD also report what each rule would hold, so the choice is made
   with the cost in view.

   A clone that wants N recent check-ins, or history from a date, names
   the check-in that expresses that and lets the rule do the rest.
   Clients SHOULD NOT express a horizon as a count or a date at the
   protocol layer; those are user-interface conveniences that resolve to
   a check-in before anything is sent.

4.  The Content Horizon

4.1.  Holdings

   A clone at a content horizon holds every structural artifact of every
   age save one kind: every check-in manifest, every sub-manifest node,
   every control artifact, and every wiki, ticket, event, forum and
   attachment artifact.  Cluster artifacts are the exception
   (Section 4.5): a horizon clone holds none, mints none and requests
   none, because a cluster enumerates artifacts of every age and a clone
   that acted on one would request what it elides.  It holds every file-
   content blob referenced by an F card of any check-in in K.  It holds
   elided phantoms for every other blob.

   Because every manifest is held, the content horizon applies to flat
   and tree-structured manifests alike.  This is the first difference
   from the sparse store of [FossilSparseCone], which requires sub-
   manifests because it elides parts of trees; a content horizon elides
   nothing a manifest is made of.

4.2.  Classification

   A blob is above the horizon if some check-in in K names it in an F
   card, directly or through a sub-manifest node, and below otherwise.
   A clone computes this over its held manifests; an origin computes it
   over the dense graph.  Both arrive at the same set because both hold
   the same manifests.




Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                 [Page 7]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   Elision is the third phantom class of [FossilSparseCone] Section 5.2,
   and every rule stated there applies: an elided phantom is
   deliberately absent, synchronization MUST NOT spontaneously request
   it, verification MUST report elision counts distinctly from damage,
   and reclassification on horizon change is recomputation, not
   migration.

   One refinement is REQUIRED.  A phantom created because a held
   artifact arrived as a delta against it ([FossilSync] Section 7.4) is
   a transit phantom regardless of its horizon classification: the clone
   cannot materialize the held artifact without it.  Section 6.2.1
   places on the origin the obligation that makes this case not arise;
   the refinement makes the clone correct when it does.

4.3.  Derived State

   The derivation of repository state ([FossilRepoState]) runs over
   structural artifacts only.  A content horizon changes none of them,
   so the derivation is byte-for-byte the dense derivation, and every
   derived view -- timeline, leaves, branch and tag state, closed
   markers, merge ancestry -- is exact over the whole history.  Derived
   caches and prefix-anchored checkpoints are horizon-independent for
   the reason [FossilSparseCone] Section 7 gives for path cones, and
   their keys MUST NOT incorporate the horizon.

4.4.  Operations That Cross the Horizon

   An operation that needs a blob below the horizon -- checking out a
   check-in below C, annotating a path back past C, diffing against an
   old check-in -- resolves the elided phantom by demand fetch
   ([FossilSparseCone] Section 6.4) in either of its two forms.  The
   targeted form fetches the exact blobs.  The policy form moves C and
   is a deepen (Section 5.8).  A content horizon has no other crossing:
   every structural question is answered locally.

4.5.  Clusters

   Cluster artifacts ([FossilArtifact]) enumerate artifacts for the
   benefit of synchronization and name blobs of every age.  A clone at a
   horizon treats cluster artifacts as elided: it MUST NOT request a
   phantom whose only reference is a cluster artifact, it does not hold
   the clusters it is sent, and it requests phantoms named by igot
   cards, by the P, F and S cards of held artifacts, and by delta
   sources of held artifacts.  An origin SHOULD NOT send cluster
   artifacts to a horizon-scoped request, because the client will not
   act on them.

5.  The Structural Horizon

5.1.  Holdings

   A clone at a structural horizon holds, of the check-in manifests and
   sub-manifest nodes, those of check-ins in K together with every
   manifest and node reachable from them through B (baseline) and S
   cards: a delta manifest's baseline may lie below C and is held
   anyway, because the delta cannot be expanded without it and a B-card
   manifest's identity is its bytes.  It holds every control artifact of
   every age, every wiki, ticket, event, forum and attachment artifact,
   and the blobs of Section 4.  It holds elided phantoms for the
   manifests and nodes below the horizon that nothing in K reaches, and
   for their blobs.  It holds one additional artifact, the seed
   (Section 5.2), which it MUST keep from every peer.

   Control artifacts are kept at every age because they are small and
   because a tag applied below the horizon can change the state in force
   at C (Section 5.6); holding them lets a clone see that it happened.

   A structural horizon REQUIRES an origin.  A clone MUST NOT enter the
   structural horizon against a horizon-ignorant peer (Section 6.3),
   because no seed is available and the derivation over the held
   manifests alone is wrong in the way Section 5.2 describes.

5.2.  The Seed

5.2.1.  What It Records

   Tag propagation ([FossilRepoState] Section 5.4) walks forward from
   the check-in a propagating tag is applied to, along primary-parent
   links.  In a dense repository, the branch tag in force at C was
   applied to some ancestor of C and propagated to it.  A clone that
   lacks C's ancestors has no artifact from which propagation can begin,
   so under the unmodified derivation every check-in in K would carry no
   branch, closed markers set below C would be invisible, and symbolic
   names applied below C would not resolve.

   The seed is a control artifact ([FossilArtifact], T card
   Section 4.7.16) minted by the origin whose T cards apply to C,
   directly, every tag that is in force at C in the dense repository:
   the propagating branch tag with its value, every other propagating
   tag with its value, the closed marker if C is closed, and every non-
   propagating tag applied to C.  Each T card carries the tag's value
   exactly as the dense derivation holds it.  The seed's D card is the
   time of minting and its U card is the origin's login.





Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                 [Page 9]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   With the seed applied, propagation begins at C with the state the
   dense repository has at C, and every check-in in K derives exactly as
   it does densely.  The seed is the only artifact a structural horizon
   holds that a dense repository does not, and the derivation needs no
   code to apply it that it does not already have.

5.2.2.  Identity and Supersession

   Tag resolution on one artifact is by timestamp: a strictly later
   mtime supersedes ([FossilRepoState] Section 5.3).  A seed minted
   later therefore supersedes an earlier seed with no special handling.
   The origin MUST set each seed's D card no earlier than the latest tag
   event it summarizes, so that a seed always outranks the direct tag
   applications it stands in for, and strictly later than any seed it
   replaces.

   A seed is a pure function of C and the dense repository's tag state
   at the time of minting.  Two origins serving one repository mint the
   same seed for the same C and the same state, up to the D and U cards.
   An origin keeps no record of which clients hold which seeds.

5.2.3.  Privacy

   A seed asserts state that the dense repository already derives from
   real tag artifacts.  Pushed into a dense repository it would be a
   redundant tag application with a late timestamp, and pushed into a
   repository whose state has moved on it would be a wrong one.  A clone
   MUST NOT send its seed to any peer under any circumstance, including
   private-content synchronization between two clones.  Two holdings are
   conforming: the seed marked private in the artifact store
   ([FossilSync] Section 7.5), or the seed held outside the artifact
   store altogether, in the clone's local derived-state tier, and
   applied to the derivation at every open exactly as a held control
   artifact would be.  The second needs no private marking because
   nothing outside the store is ever offered to a peer, and it is what
   an implementation without a private-artifact store does.  An origin
   MUST refuse a seed offered to it.  A seed is identified as such by
   the origin's capability of Section 6.2.5 and by its presence in a
   reply to the horizon pragma; a clone that receives an artifact
   matching the seed's shape by any other route treats it as an ordinary
   control artifact.

5.2.4.  Provenance

   An origin that holds a signing key SHOULD sign the seed using the
   clear-signing envelope that [FossilArtifact] Section 4 already
   defines for artifacts, so that a clone can distinguish a seed its
   origin minted from one substituted in transit or on disk.  A clone
   that knows its origin's public key MUST verify the signature of every
   seed it holds and MUST discard an unsigned or wrongly signed seed
   from such an origin, falling back to the content horizon
   (Section 6.3) rather than holding derived state it cannot attribute.
   A clone with no key for its origin accepts an unsigned seed.

   A signature proves provenance, not correctness: an origin that
   derives wrongly signs its wrong derivation.  Because a client MUST
   already verify that a seed's bytes hash to the name the origin
   announced in the same reply (Section 6.2.3), substitution requires
   control of the connection itself, which the transport's own
   protections address; the signature is what lets a clone keep trusting
   a seed after the connection is gone, when the seed is read back from
   disk or carried into a deployment the transport never covered.
   Section 5.9 states what a wrong seed can and cannot affect.

5.3.  Boundary Hashes

   A check-in in K whose parent is below the horizon names that parent
   in a P card.  The clone cannot tell, from the hash alone, whether an
   unheld parent is below the horizon or a new check-in it has not yet
   received.  The origin resolves this by labelling: alongside the
   manifests it sends for a structural-horizon request it names every
   boundary hash (Section 6.2.2).  A clone classifies a labelled hash as
   an elided phantom and an unlabelled unheld reference as a transit
   phantom, which it requests.

   When a new check-in forks from below the horizon, its parent becomes
   a new boundary hash, and the origin labels it in the round that
   delivers the check-in.  A clone that receives a check-in whose parent
   is unheld and unlabelled requests the parent; this is the correct
   behavior when the parent is merely late and a bounded over-fetch when
   the origin omitted a label.

5.4.  Derived State

   With the seed and the boundary labels in place, the derivation runs
   unmodified over the held artifacts.  Leaves are exact: a leaf is a
   check-in no held check-in names as parent, and every child of a
   check-in in K is in K.  Branch and tag state is exact for every
   check-in in K.  The timeline is exact above C and ends at the
   boundary hashes.  Merge ancestry is exact within K.



Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                [Page 11]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   A clone SHOULD render a boundary hash as the horizon rather than as a
   missing parent, and MUST NOT describe it as damage.  Verification
   MUST report manifests below the horizon as elided, distinctly from
   damage, as [FossilSparseCone] Section 5.2 requires for every elided
   class.

5.5.  Operations That Cross the Horizon

   The following operations can need history below the horizon.  For
   each, an implementation MUST detect the crossing, MUST name the
   horizon in its diagnostic, and MUST offer the remedy stated.

   Merge base:  Under the leaves rule the merge base of any two live
      branches is in K; under the descendants rule it is in K only for
      branches forked at or after C.  When a merge's base is below the
      horizon, the clone asks the origin for it (Section 6.2.4) and
      deepens to it.  The merge then runs locally over real artifacts.
      An implementation MUST NOT merge against a base it does not hold.

   Symbolic names:  A tag applied to a check-in below the horizon
      resolves to that check-in's hash, because the control artifact is
      held.  Operating on the check-in is a deepen.

   Checkout, diff, annotate below C:  A deepen, or the targeted demand
      fetch of [FossilSparseCone] Section 6.4 for the blobs and
      manifests needed.  An origin that offers a history interface
      outside the sync protocol MAY be named in the capability
      advertisement (Section 6.2.5) so a client can direct the user to
      it; the interface itself is out of scope here.

   Bisect:  A bisection whose good end is below the horizon deepens to
      it before it begins.

5.6.  Re-Seeding

   A tag applied to a check-in below the horizon changes the state in
   force at C when, and only when, it is a propagating tag (or a
   cancellation of one) whose propagation reaches C along primary-parent
   links.  The clone holds the control artifact and so can see that a
   tag event occurred below the horizon, but it cannot compute whether
   the event reaches C.

   A clone therefore requests the seed at each synchronization
   (Section 6.2.3), naming the seed it holds.  The origin recomputes the
   seed for C and replies with a new one only when it differs.  Because
   supersession is by timestamp, applying the new seed is an ordinary
   tag application.  The origin keeps no per-client state to do this;
   the request carries everything it needs.

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


5.7.  Commit and Push

   A commit from a structural horizon is an ordinary commit.  Its
   manifest names its parent by real hash, names blobs by real hash, and
   carries the F cards or S cards of its tree; nothing in it reveals the
   horizon.  A check-in on an existing branch writes no branch tag; the
   branch is inherited by propagation at every peer from that peer's own
   derivation.  A check-in that creates a branch writes the T cards that
   [FossilRepoState] specifies, including the cancellation of the parent
   branch's symbolic tag.  That cancellation names the parent branch as
   the clone derived it, which is the one place a clone's derived state
   enters an artifact.  An origin SHOULD check the cancelled name
   against its own derivation at push and, on disagreement, refuse the
   push with a diagnostic naming the seed as stale and the branch the
   parent is on.

   History is append-only, so the check-in cannot be unmade; the remedy
   is a control artifact.  After re-synchronizing, which brings the tag
   that moved the parent and so re-seeds the clone, the client writes a
   control artifact that cancels the parent's real branch tag on the
   check-in (the tag move [FossilRepoState] Section 6.2 specifies, which
   a "move to branch" command already writes) and pushes again.  An
   origin MUST admit a check-in whose cancellation it would otherwise
   refuse when the same push, or its own store, carries a control
   artifact cancelling the parent's branch tag on that check-in; the
   correction makes the derived result at every peer what the client
   intended.

   Pushing is ordinary.  The origin holds every artifact the pushed
   check-in references, because the clone's held set came from the
   origin and the clone added only what it created.  A structural
   horizon clone pushing to a peer that is not its origin and lacks the
   referenced ancestry leaves transit phantoms at that peer, exactly as
   [FossilSparseCone] Section 5.3 describes for sparse stores.

5.8.  Deepen

   To deepen, a clone names a new C' that is an ancestor of C,
   recomputes K, and performs a horizon-scoped synchronization at C'.
   The origin sends the manifests, nodes and blobs that the new K adds,
   labels the new boundary hashes, and sends the seed for C'.  Nothing
   already held is replaced, and nothing already held is trusted
   differently: the seed for C becomes inert, because propagation from
   the seed at C' reaches C through real manifests and the later-dated
   direct application at C would otherwise shadow it.  To prevent that
   shadow, a deepening clone MUST discard the old seed before applying
   the new one; a seed is local and private, so discarding it is a local
   deletion with no synchronization consequence.



Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                [Page 13]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   Deepen is the universal remedy of this document.  Every operation in
   Section 5.5 resolves to it or to a targeted fetch.

5.9.  Trust

   A seed is derived state the clone did not derive.  Its blast radius
   is bounded as follows.

   A wrong seed misplaces check-ins in K on branches, misreports closed
   state, and misresolves symbolic names, in the clone's local view
   only.  Ordinary commits carry no branch card and are unaffected.
   Branch creation carries a cancellation card derived from the seed;
   the push-time check above catches a wrong one at the origin, so the
   shared repository is protected by the origin's own derivation over
   real artifacts.

   A seed is verifiable by deepening.  The branch tag in force at C was
   applied to some ancestor A of C and propagated; a clone that deepens
   to A holds the real tag artifact and the real primary-parent chain,
   and can recompute what the seed asserted.  The same holds for every
   tag the seed carries.  An implementation SHOULD offer this as a
   verification mode, so that trusting the seed is a choice with a known
   price rather than an assumption.

   A signature (Section 5.2) lets a clone reject a seed its origin did
   not mint, including one altered on disk after delivery.  It does not
   make a seed true.

6.  Sync Protocol Extension

   Every card this section defines is a pragma, for the reason
   [FossilSparseCone] Section 6.1 gives: the reference implementation
   ignores unknown pragmas and rejects unknown bare cards, so pragmas
   are what make the degradation of Section 6.3 a documented property
   rather than an assumption.

6.1.  The Horizon Pragmas

   A horizon-scoped pull or clone request carries:

   pragma horizon <hash> <rule>

   where <hash> is the full artifact hash of C and <rule> is descendants
   or leaves.  At most one horizon pragma appears in a request.  A
   request MAY also carry the path-cone pragmas of [FossilSparseCone]
   Section 6.1; the keep set is then the intersection stated in
   Section 3.




Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                [Page 14]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   A request for the structural horizon adds:

   pragma horizon-structural

   Absent this pragma, the request is for the content horizon, and the
   origin sends every structural artifact.

   Any request MAY carry the cursor of Section 6.2.6:

   pragma horizon-cursor <n>

6.2.  Server Behavior

   An origin receiving a horizon pragma computes K over the dense graph
   and restricts the content it sends to blobs above the horizon.  For a
   structural request it further restricts manifests and sub-manifest
   nodes to those of check-ins in K, continues to send every control,
   wiki, ticket, event, forum and attachment artifact, and sends the
   boundary labels and the seed.  The origin MUST bound the per-round
   walk exactly as a dense clone bounds its batches.

   The classification is a function of the cone and of the artifacts the
   origin holds, and every set it computes grows monotonically as
   artifacts are appended: a check-in that enters K never leaves it, a
   blob named above the horizon stays above, a manifest K reaches stays
   reached.  The one exception is an artifact that arrives after an
   artifact already held cited it as a parent, baseline or node, which
   can bring older check-ins into K.  An origin SHOULD therefore keep
   the classification keyed by the cone digest and the prefix of its
   store it classified, extend it on each request by classifying only
   the artifacts appended since, and recompute it in full only when an
   appended artifact carries a name a held P, B or S card cited while it
   was absent.  A blob arriving after the manifest that names it needs
   no recomputation: classified as a holding nothing references, it is
   kept, which is more than the exact answer and never less.  Without
   this an origin re-reads its store on every request that follows a
   push, and a store of a few hundred thousand check-ins answers a one-
   artifact pull in minutes.

   The references a classification reads -- each structural artifact's
   kind and the artifacts its P, B, S, F and A cards name -- do not
   depend on the cone either.  An origin SHOULD read them once per store
   into a graph keyed to the same prefix, extend that graph by the
   artifacts appended since, and classify every cone, including one it
   has never seen, over the graph rather than over the artifacts.  The
   first classification of a store then costs one read of its structural
   artifacts; every later one, for any horizon, costs a walk over what
   that read produced.



Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                [Page 15]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   An origin SHOULD NOT send cluster artifacts in reply to a horizon-
   scoped request.

6.2.1.  Delta Inflation

   A stored artifact may be held in delta form against a base
   ([FossilDelta]) and a dense clone may serve such a container
   verbatim, because a dense recipient holds the base.  A horizon
   recipient may not.  An origin MUST NOT send, in reply to a horizon-
   scoped request, a delta whose base is not in the request's keep set;
   it MUST send such an artifact in full form.  An origin MAY continue
   to send deltas whose base is in the keep set verbatim.

   The check is per served delta and is a membership test against the
   keep set; reconstruction is required only for the deltas that cross
   it.  An origin SHOULD bound the reconstruction work in a round the
   way it bounds the walk, and MAY defer crossing deltas to a later
   round rather than exceed its bound.  An origin that cannot
   reconstruct a crossing artifact at all (its content does not inflate,
   or exceeds the reply body) MAY send the stored delta verbatim rather
   than omit the artifact: the recipient's transit rule (Section 4.2)
   then fetches the base, which costs bandwidth and never a stuck
   artifact, where an omission would be silent.

6.2.2.  Boundary Labels

   For a structural request the origin emits, for every boundary hash
   that the manifests in the reply introduce:

   pragma horizon-below <hash>

   A client classifies the named hash as an elided phantom
   (Section 5.3).  The origin MUST emit the label in the same reply as
   the first manifest that names the hash, or in an earlier one.

6.2.3.  The Seed

   A structural request carries:

   pragma horizon-seed <hash-or-dash>

   naming the seed the client currently holds, or - if it holds none.
   The origin computes the seed for C.  If its name differs from the one
   offered, the origin emits:

   pragma horizon-seed <hash>





Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                [Page 16]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   naming the new seed, and sends it in the same reply as an ordinary
   full file card ([FossilSync] Section 7.1) whose HASH is that name.
   The pragma MUST precede the card.  A client holding a horizon-seed
   name from the current reply routes the file card bearing it to its
   seed holding (Section 5.2) instead of its artifact store, and never
   announces or sends it.  The client MUST verify that the card's bytes
   hash to the announced name and parse as a control artifact before
   holding them, and MUST drop a card that fails either test: otherwise
   an origin could divert any artifact out of the store by naming it.
   An announcement is good for the reply that made it only.  If the
   names agree the origin emits nothing.  A file card whose hash no
   horizon-seed pragma in the same reply named is an ordinary artifact.

   An origin MUST NOT advertise the structural level (Section 6.2.5)
   unless it is sending a seed in the same reply or the client's offered
   name is the seed it would send; a structural request it cannot seed
   is answered, and advertised, as content.  A seed's D card SHOULD be
   the latest mtime among the tag events it summarizes, not the time of
   minting, so that two mints over unchanged state produce the same
   bytes and the same name and the offered name matches; the requirement
   of Section 5.2.2 that a re-minted seed carry a later D card is then
   met by construction, because a re-mint happens only when a later tag
   event exists.

6.2.4.  Merge Base

   A client MAY ask:

   pragma horizon-mergebase <hash-a> <hash-b>

   and an origin that can answer replies:

   pragma horizon-mergebase <hash-a> <hash-b> <hash-base>

   or, when no common ancestor exists, with - as the third hash.  The
   client then deepens to the base.  An origin MUST bound the ancestry
   walk this requires.

6.2.5.  Capability

   An origin that implements this document emits in every reply to a
   request carrying a horizon pragma:

   pragma horizon-served <level> [<url>]

   where <level> is content or structural, naming the deepest cut the
   origin serves, and the optional <url> names an interface outside this
   protocol at which history below a horizon can be queried.  A client



Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                [Page 17]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   MUST NOT enter the structural horizon unless the reply to its first
   round carried horizon-served structural.  A client SHOULD report
   which mode a transfer used.

6.2.6.  The Cursor

   A dense pull announces every artifact the client holds with igot so
   the origin can withhold what the client has, and a push does the same
   so the origin can request what it lacks.  Under a horizon the
   client's holding is a cut of the origin's, the origin knows exactly
   what it sent, and the announcement is the largest part of a request
   that otherwise carries nothing: one card per held artifact, on every
   pull.

   An origin that classified the request emits in every reply:

   pragma horizon-cursor <n>

   where <n> is the position in the origin's own artifact sequence, in
   the sense of the clone SEQNO of [FossilSync] Section 8.2, through
   which this reply was served: every artifact the origin held at or
   below <n> that the classification keeps has been announced or sent to
   a client that has converged against this reply.  A client that
   converged records the cursor of the last reply together with the
   count of its own artifacts at the end of the exchange, per origin.

   A client holding a cursor for the origin it addresses sends it on
   every request, and MAY then announce nothing with igot on a pull-only
   request.  On a request that sends it MUST still announce, but MAY
   announce only the artifacts it appended after the recorded count,
   since every older artifact either came from this origin or was
   announced to it by a prior converged exchange that sent.  The
   recorded count therefore advances only past artifacts the origin
   holds: a pull sends nothing of the client's, so it advances the count
   by what it received, and only when nothing undelivered preceded them;
   a client without a cursor announces its whole holding on its next
   request that sends.  An origin receiving a
   cursor MUST NOT announce an artifact at or below it in any reply,
   classified or not, and MUST announce every kept artifact above it the
   request did not name.  A push reply is where this matters most: the
   request carries no horizon, so without the cursor an origin announces
   every holding no cluster names, which on a store that has never
   minted a cluster is its whole inventory.

   The cursor is only as good as the classification it was handed out
   from.  When an artifact arrives that a held artifact already cited
   (Section 6.2), the origin classifies the cone afresh and records the
   position at which it did so; a request carrying a cursor below that
   position is answered as if it carried none, once, and the reply's
   cursor moves the client past it.

   The cursor changes what is announced, never what is answered: an
   origin MUST answer a gimme for any artifact its classification keeps
   whatever cursor the request carried, and a client MUST request every
   phantom it wants exactly as without a cursor.  A kept artifact at or
   below the cursor that the client lacks is therefore still obtained
   the moment the client learns its name, from a manifest that cites it.
   The cursor is advisory on both sides: a client that lost it announces
   fully; an origin that cannot place it ignores it.  A client that
   suspects its holding is incomplete MAY omit the cursor to force a
   full announcement.

6.3.  Graceful Degradation

   A horizon-ignorant peer ignores the pragmas and serves densely.  For
   a content-horizon request the client MUST then classify locally: it
   holds every manifest, so it computes K and the keep set itself,
   stores the blobs above the horizon, records elided phantoms for the
   remainder, and reports the counts.  Correctness is preserved;
   bandwidth is lost.  The delta-inflation rule cannot be enforced
   against such a peer, and the transit refinement of Section 4.2 is
   what keeps the clone correct when a kept artifact arrives as a delta
   against an elided base: the base is fetched.

   For a structural-horizon request against a horizon-ignorant peer
   there is no seed and no labelling.  The client MUST fall back to the
   content horizon for that transfer and MUST report that it did.

6.4.  Not a Complete Origin

   A horizon clone cannot serve as a clone origin for a request it does
   not cover, for the reason [FossilSparseCone] Section 5.3 gives.  A
   server over a horizon clone MUST refuse a dense clone request and
   MUST refuse a horizon request whose K is not a subset of its own,
   with one error card and no content, and a requesting client MUST
   treat that reply as fatal exactly as that section requires.

7.  Interactions

   This section is informative.

   Path cones: compose by intersection on blobs, as Section 3 states.  A
   path cone never elides structure, so a path-cone-plus-content-horizon
   clone still derives exactly, and a path-cone-plus-structural-horizon
   clone derives exactly within K.

   Sub-manifests ([FossilSubManifests]): under a content horizon every
   node is held.  Under a structural horizon the nodes of check-ins in K
   are held and the rest are elided phantoms; nodes shared across the
   horizon by unchanged subtrees are above it, because some check-in in
   K references them.

   Shunning: unchanged.  A shunned artifact is never requested
   regardless of its horizon classification.

   Hybrid histories: a content horizon applies to the flat prefix of a
   hybrid history, because it holds manifests whole.  A structural
   horizon applies to it as well, for the same reason; nothing in this
   document requires sub-manifests.




Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                [Page 19]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   Derived caches: under a content horizon, nothing changes.  Under a
   structural horizon, the local log holds a different set of manifests
   than a dense log, and a prefix-anchored checkpoint over it is a
   checkpoint over that log; it remains horizon-independent in the sense
   that it derives from the bytes present and needs no horizon term in
   its key.

8.  Security Considerations

   The seed is the only element of this document that asks a clone to
   hold derived state it did not derive.  Section 5.9 bounds what a
   wrong seed can affect and states how a clone verifies one.  The hash
   check binds a seed to the reply that delivered it; the signature
   (Section 5.2) binds it to the origin, which is the property that
   survives the connection.  A deployment whose clones are read from
   disk by other tools, or whose origin is reached through
   intermediaries, should treat the signature as required rather than
   recommended.  Deployments in which a clone cannot accept its origin's
   derivation of branch state should use the content horizon, which
   holds no such state.

   A seed is private and MUST NOT leave the clone.  A clone that
   synchronizes private content with another clone ([FossilSync]
   Section 7.5) MUST exclude the seed from that exchange.

   The horizon pragmas enlarge the request-parse surface by a bounded
   number of fixed-arity cards.  The held-set computation on the origin
   is an ancestry walk; it MUST carry the same depth and visited-node
   bounds as any other server-side graph walk, and the merge-base query
   MUST be bounded the same way, so that a crafted request does not
   convert an unauthenticated round into an amplification primitive.
   Delta inflation on the serve path is work proportional to the
   crossing deltas in a round and MUST be bounded by the round's batch
   limit.

   A horizon discloses to the origin which part of history a clone uses.
   Hosted deployments should treat horizons as information about their
   users, as [FossilSparseCone] Section 8 says of cones.

Appendix A.  Implementation Sequencing

   This appendix is informative and binds nothing.  A dependency-ordered
   implementation:

   1.  Delta inflation for crossing bases (Section 6.2.1), cluster
       suppression, and the transit refinement of Section 4.2.  These
       correct the path-cone serve path as well and ship before any
       horizon does.

   2.  The horizon member of the cone, its serialization, and the
       content horizon over held manifests (Section 3, Section 4).
       Correct against every peer; efficient against an origin.

   3.  The horizon pragmas and the origin's keep-set classification
       (Section 6.1, Section 6.2).

   4.  Measurement.  The structural horizon pays only when the manifests
       below C are a meaningful share of what a content horizon still
       transfers.  That share is a property of the repository, not of
       this document.

   5.  The seed, boundary labels, re-seeding, and the structural horizon
       (Section 5), gated on the measurement.

   6.  The merge-base query and deepen as a verb (Section 6.2.4,
       Section 5.8); the push-time cancellation check; the verification
       mode of Section 5.9.

Appendix B.  Consumers

   This appendix is informative.  Three consumers want a cheap copy of a
   repository at its tip, and they do not all want a clone.

   A build-and-test job wants the tree at one check-in.  An archive of
   that check-in, served by the origin, costs the bytes of a checkout
   and involves no history, no phantoms and no derivation.  It is the
   right tool and this document does not improve on it.





Mestas                    Expires 20 April 2027                [Page 22]

Internet-Draft               Fossil Horizons                October 2026


   A persistent worker wants to run many jobs against one repository.  A
   dense clone kept warm on the worker and synchronized forward, with
   each job given a copy-on-write copy of it, pays the clone once per
   host.

   A cold, single-use environment that must commit and push needs a real
   repository and has no warm copy.  It is the consumer of this
   document: the content horizon first, the structural horizon when the
   manifests below C are what remains worth removing.
